# Canadian Scam Red Flags & Anti-Fraud Protection

Cyber-enabled financial scams targeting Canadian post-secondary students and young newcomers have surged. Understanding common attacker tactics protects your savings and identity.

## Common Canadian Fraud Patterns (MITRE F3 Mapped)
1. **Fake Interac e-Transfer Notifications:**
   - **Tactic:** Adversary sends an SMS or email stating: *"You received $240 from OSAP/CRA Refund. Claim now at bit.ly/..."*
   - **Reality:** Legitimate Interac e-Transfers are either deposited automatically via Autodeposit or direct you to your bank's official portal. The CRA, OSAP, and Service Canada **never** send funds or refund notifications via text message links.
2. **Government Agency Impersonation (CRA / OSAP / Service Canada / 407 ETR):**
   - **Tactic:** Threat actors threaten arrest, police dispatch, suspension of SIN, or immediate licence revocation unless overdue taxes or highway tolls are paid within 24 hours.
   - **Reality:** Canadian federal agencies never demand instant payment via Bitcoin ATMs, iTunes/prepaid gift cards, or Interac e-Transfers, nor do they send SMS links with shortened URLs (`bit.ly`, `tinyurl`, `t.co`, `rb.gy`). Official CRA communication arrives by mail or directly inside your verified CRA My Account inbox.
3. **Rental Deposit & Advance-Fee Scams:**
   - **Tactic:** Fake landlord lists an attractive, underpriced student apartment on Facebook Marketplace or Kijiji, claiming they are out of the country and demanding a first-and-last month e-Transfer deposit before scheduling an in-person viewing or providing keys.
   - **Reality:** Never transfer money before inspecting a rental property in person and verifying the lease agreement.
4. **Remote Job Cheque-Cashing & Mystery Shopper Scams:**
   - **Tactic:** Student receives an unsolicited remote job offer and is sent a fraudulent digital cheque for $2,500 to buy home-office equipment, with instructions to deposit via mobile banking and wire back the remaining balance.
   - **Reality:** When the fraudulent cheque bounces 3 to 5 business days later, the student is personally liable to their bank for the entire sum.

## Exact Steps If You Encounter a Scam
1. **Do not click links** or reply to the message.
2. **Report the incident** to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC) online or toll-free at 1-888-495-8501.
3. If financial or card info was shared, call your bank's fraud department immediately to freeze accounts and debit cards.
