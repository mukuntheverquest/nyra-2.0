# Understanding Ontario Paycheques, Deductions & Taxes (2026)

When you receive your first paycheque in Ontario, your net deposit is significantly lower than your nominal hourly rate times hours worked. Understanding statutory payroll deductions ensures realistic cash-flow planning.

## Gross Pay vs. Net Take-Home Pay
- **Gross Pay:** Total earnings before any taxes or mandatory contributions are deducted ($hourly wage \times hours worked$).
- **Net Pay:** The actual dollars deposited into your bank account.

## Mandatory 2026 Payroll Deductions
Every employer in Ontario is legally required to withhold three statutory items:
1. **Canada Pension Plan (CPP):**
   - Base employee contribution rate is **5.95%** on pensionable earnings above the **$3,500 basic annual exemption**.
   - For 2026, the Year’s Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YMPE) is **$74,600** (maximum base employee contribution: **$4,230.45**).
   - Second CPP tier (CPP2): Applied at **4.0%** on earnings between the YMPE ($74,600) and the Year’s Additional Maximum Pensionable Earnings (YAMPE: **$85,000**), up to a 2026 maximum of **$416.00**.
2. **Employment Insurance (EI):**
   - For 2026, the employee premium rate outside Quebec is **1.63%**.
   - Maximum Insurable Earnings (MIE) is **$68,900**, capping annual employee premiums at **$1,123.07**.
3. **Income Tax (Federal & Ontario):**
   - **Federal Tax:** The 2026 Federal Basic Personal Amount (BPA) is **$16,452**. The lowest federal tax bracket applies at **14.0%** on taxable income up to **$58,523**.
   - **Ontario Provincial Tax:** The 2026 Ontario Basic Personal Amount is **$12,989**. The lowest Ontario tax bracket applies at **5.05%** on taxable income up to **$53,891**.

## Why You Must File a Tax Return (Even on Low/Zero Income)
Filing an annual personal tax return (T1) using your T4 slips is vital for youth and students:
- Unlocks quarterly **GST/HST credit** direct deposits.
- Unlocks the **Ontario Trillium Benefit (OTB)**, including the Ontario Energy and Property Tax Credit for rent paid in Ontario.
- Accumulates RRSP deduction limit and carries forward post-secondary tuition tax credits.
